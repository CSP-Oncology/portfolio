<section class="section wrap">
  <div class="grid grid--2">
    <div class="note">
      <h2>The short version</h2>
      <p>RELOAD is a blood test, still in development, that looks for a sneaky trick some cancers use to hide from the body's immune system. Knowing about that trick helps doctors predict whether a treatment called <strong>immunotherapy</strong> is likely to work, so they can plan care sooner.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="card">
      <h2>What does the name mean?</h2>
      <p><strong>R</strong>estriction <strong>E</strong>lement <strong>L</strong>oss <strong>O</strong>f <strong>A</strong>llele <strong>D</strong>etection</p>
      <ul class="check-list">
        <li><strong>Restriction element:</strong> a name immunologists use for HLA, the "display" molecules explained below</li>
        <li><strong>Loss of allele:</strong> the cancer has lost one of its two copies of an HLA gene</li>
        <li><strong>Detection:</strong> the test finds that loss</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section wrap">
  <h2>The background, in four steps</h2>
  <p>You don't need a biology degree. Here is the idea with an everyday comparison.</p>
  <ol class="steps">
    <li><div><strong>Your immune system is a security team</strong><span>Immune cells called T cells patrol your body and check other cells for signs that something is wrong, such as a cancer.</span></div></li>
    <li><div><strong>Cells hold up samples for inspection</strong><span>Every cell has display molecules called HLA on its surface. Think of them as little hands holding up samples of what is inside the cell so the T cells can inspect them. Cancer cells hold up abnormal samples, and that is how T cells spot them.</span></div></li>
    <li><div><strong>Some cancers hide by dropping a display</strong><span>We inherit two copies of each HLA gene, one from each parent. Some tumors lose one copy. This is called <strong>HLA LOH</strong> (loss of heterozygosity). With fewer displays, the tumor shows fewer abnormal samples, so it is harder for T cells to catch.</span></div></li>
    <li><div><strong>Immunotherapy needs the tumor to be visible</strong><span>Immunotherapy drugs work by releasing the brakes on T cells so they attack the cancer. If the tumor is hiding, the drugs tend to work less well. Doctors would like to know that before choosing a treatment.</span></div></li>
  </ol>
</section>

<section class="section wrap">
  <div class="grid grid--2">
    <div class="card">
      <h2>Why a blood test?</h2>
      <p>Finding out whether a tumor has lost an HLA copy normally means taking a piece of the tumor, called a biopsy. That can be hard, uncomfortable, or sometimes not possible. Tumors also shed tiny bits of their DNA into the bloodstream, so a simple blood draw can pick up the same information. This kind of test is called a <strong>liquid biopsy</strong>.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="card">
      <h2>How RELOAD works</h2>
      <ol class="steps">
        <li><div><strong>Blood draw</strong><span>A small blood sample is taken, like a routine blood test.</span></div></li>
        <li><div><strong>Collect the DNA</strong><span>Free-floating DNA in the blood (cell-free DNA, or cfDNA) is collected. Some of it came from the tumor.</span></div></li>
        <li><div><strong>Read the DNA</strong><span>A machine reads the DNA code, a method called next-generation sequencing.</span></div></li>
        <li><div><strong>Look for the missing copy</strong><span>The results are checked for signs that the tumor lost one of its HLA copies.</span></div></li>
        <li><div><strong>Report to the doctor</strong><span>The result helps the oncology team decide on treatment.</span></div></li>
      </ol>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section wrap">
  <div class="grid grid--2">
    <div class="card">
      <h2>Where RELOAD stands today</h2>
      <ul class="check-list">
        <li><strong>Made by Sesh:</strong> a San Diego precision-oncology startup</li>
        <li><strong>Research roots:</strong> the technology was licensed from the U.S. National Cancer Institute (NCI)</li>
        <li><strong>Funded first step:</strong> a Phase I NCI small-business grant, focused on non-small cell lung cancer, where HLA loss is common</li>
        <li><strong>Next:</strong> studies to prove the test is accurate and reliable</li>
        <li><strong>Long-term goal:</strong> FDA approval</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
    <div class="note">
      <h2>Important to know</h2>
      <p>RELOAD is early-stage. It is <strong>not yet FDA-approved</strong> and is not something patients can ask for today. The published information about it is limited, so some details, such as exactly how the lab analysis works, are not public.</p>
    </div>

<section class="section wrap">
  <div class="card">
    <h2>Key words</h2>
    <ul class="check-list">
      <li><strong>Biomarker:</strong> a measurable sign in the body that gives doctors information, such as whether a treatment will work</li>
      <li><strong>Immunotherapy:</strong> cancer treatment that helps the immune system fight the tumor</li>
      <li><strong>HLA:</strong> display molecules on cells that show samples to immune cells</li>
      <li><strong>HLA LOH:</strong> a tumor has lost one of its two copies of an HLA gene</li>
      <li><strong>Liquid biopsy:</strong> testing blood instead of taking a piece of the tumor</li>
      <li><strong>cfDNA:</strong> DNA floating freely in the blood</li>
      <li><strong>Sequencing:</strong> reading the letters of the DNA code</li>
      <li><strong>NSCLC:</strong> non-small cell lung cancer, the most common type of lung cancer</li>
    </ul>
